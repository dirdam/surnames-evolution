    How it works

    1. It starts with 10,000 people, with each surname's share proportional to its real-world ranking.
    2. Every simulated year, everyone ages by one year.
    3. People aged 15–35 may marry and have children — the more children a couple already has, the less likely they are to have another. Children inherit their father's surname.
    4. Everyone has a chance of dying each year, based on real death-rate-by-age data.
    5. The simulation ends when either one surname takes over everyone, or a thousand years pass with the same surnames still around.

    No matter how many surnames there are, if only the father's surname is ever passed down, there will always eventually come a day when just one remains.
